And what did I actually learn?

Seven weeks is not long. It was enough.

  1. 01

    AI is leverage, not a shortcut

    Week three, Kevin told me once you get the hang of MCP and API keys, your ai capabilities will 10x. He was right. The trick was never asking it to do the thinking — it was knowing exactly what I wanted before I asked, and then having it done in an afternoon instead of a fortnight.

  2. 02

    Taste is the actual job

    The first batch of X posts got the note feel really ai generated atm, and that note was correct. Anyone can generate a draft now. Knowing which draft is bad, and being willing to write it again until it isn't, is the part that's still yours.

  3. 03

    Writing the manual is how you learn the product

    Nobody sat me down and taught me Spott. I learned it by moving the docs out of Notion one page at a time and having to explain every screen to a stranger. You find out precisely how much you understand the moment you have to write it down.

  4. 04

    Startup mindset is just less friction

    it slows you down needing to log out → thats not startup mindset ;) is the most Kevin sentence ever typed, and it turned out to be about more than logging out. Take the small obstacle away, now, rather than working around it politely for six weeks.

  5. 05

    You can just start

    I came in not knowing what an ATS was, or a repo, or roughly forty percent of Kevin's Slack messages. Nobody was going to hand me permission to build, film, ship or deploy. You pick it up by doing it badly first, in public, on a deadline.

Thank you for everything.

Seven weeks ago I did not know what an ATS was, what a repository was, or what Kevin meant by roughly forty percent of his Slack messages. I am leaving able to build, write, film, ship and deploy — and with a genuinely unreasonable fondness for all of you.
